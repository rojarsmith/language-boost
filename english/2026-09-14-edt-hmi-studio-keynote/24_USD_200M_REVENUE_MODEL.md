# US$200M repeatable annual revenue model

## Decision status

**Scenario only. Not approved guidance.**

This document shows one transparent way EDT HMI Studio could support a US$200M annual revenue run-rate. It does not claim that EDT will reach the number, that the revenue has been contracted, or that Finance has approved the assumptions.

Use the term **repeatable annual revenue engine**. Do not call the total software ARR. Most of the illustrative total comes from repeated annual display production.

## The equation

| Revenue engine | Illustrative volume | Illustrative annual revenue per unit | Annual revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A. Production display programs | 80 active programs | US$2.0M per program | US$160M |
| B. AI, support, and enterprise services | 2,000 paid enterprise teams | US$10K per team | US$20M |
| C. Strategic OEM and ecosystem agreements | 10 agreements | US$2.0M per agreement | US$20M |
| **Total annual run-rate** |  |  | **US$200M** |

Formula:

> 80 × US$2.0M + 2,000 × US$10K + 10 × US$2.0M = US$200M

Every input is an assumption that requires evidence. The equation is useful because a leader can challenge one line without rejecting the whole strategy.

## Engine A: production display programs

### Mechanism

Studio gives customers a working path from concept to a supported EDT display. A customer that finishes the HMI and validates the board around an EDT profile has more reasons to keep the same display and EDT support relationship through production and later product variants.

### Revenue included

- Annual production shipments of EDT display modules for active design-ins
- Repeat orders and approved product variants within those programs

### Revenue excluded

- Software and service fees counted in Engine B
- One-time licenses counted in Engine C
- Pipeline, samples, evaluation kits, and unsigned forecasts

### What must be true

- EDT can create enough qualified design-ins.
- The average production program generates about US$2.0M each year.
- Programs stay in production long enough to create repeat orders.
- Studio materially improves conversion, speed, retention, or account access.
- Supply, margin, quality, and support capacity can sustain the volume.

### Metrics

- Qualified Studio evaluations
- Evaluation-to-design-in conversion
- Time from evaluation to nomination
- Active production programs
- Annual display revenue per active program
- Program retention and repeat-generation rate
- Gross margin and FAE hours per design-in

## Engine B: AI, support, and enterprise services

### Possible offers

- Managed AI usage and administration
- Enterprise support and response commitments
- Training, enablement, and certification
- Private deployment or approved local-model integration
- Advanced collaboration, governance, or fleet tooling if productized

These offers are concepts. Product, Security, Legal, Finance, and channel leadership must define the actual package.

### What must be true

- A paid team receives measurable value beyond the base software.
- EDT can bill, support, secure, and renew the service.
- OpenRouter or other external-provider costs leave acceptable margin.
- Local deployment requirements have a supportable architecture.
- Paid teams do not double-count revenue already included in strategic agreements.

### Metrics

- Activated teams and monthly active teams
- Paid conversion and annual contract value
- AI cost per paid team
- Support load and gross margin
- Renewal and expansion rate
- Security and privacy review cycle

## Engine C: strategic OEM and ecosystem agreements

### Possible counterparties

- HMI vendors
- MCU and SoC vendors
- Large equipment makers
- Regional partners with a defined platform role

### Possible commercial structure

- Annual technology license
- Co-development and maintenance agreement
- Platform integration and enablement fee
- Minimum annual commitment linked to a supported product family

Do not present these structures as current offers until Legal and Finance approve them.

### What must be true

- EDT owns or can license every required software component.
- The agreement does not weaken EDT display design-ins or create channel conflict.
- Support and roadmap obligations are measurable.
- Ten counterparties with an average annual value of US$2.0M are realistically addressable.

### Metrics

- Qualified strategic accounts
- Technical integrations completed
- Contracted annual value
- Renewal term and support cost
- Incremental display pull-through

## Why the revenue can repeat

The word repeatable depends on three different mechanisms:

| Engine | Repeat mechanism |
|---|---|
| Production display programs | Customers reorder hardware while products remain in production |
| AI and enterprise services | Customers renew access, support, or managed usage |
| Strategic agreements | Counterparties renew licenses, maintenance, or annual commitments |

This is weaker than contractually committed recurring revenue unless contracts support it. The keynote must say **repeatable annual revenue**, not guaranteed recurring revenue.

## Product-to-revenue chain

| Product action | Customer outcome to validate | Commercial result to measure |
|---|---|---|
| AI creates or edits an HMI | Faster first working concept | More qualified evaluations |
| Visual logic and protocol setup | Fewer handoffs and earlier integration | Higher evaluation-to-design-in conversion |
| Emulator and Customer Demo | Stakeholders can approve before hardware is ready | Shorter evaluation cycle |
| Board-aware build and deployment | Clearer path to supported EDT hardware | More EDT module nominations |
| Reusable project and profiles | Easier variants and later generations | Longer account and program retention |
| Enterprise support and governance | Lower adoption risk for large teams | Paid service conversion and renewal |

None of these causal links should be presented as proven until EDT has cohort data.

## Required validation

### Gate 1: demand

- Interview at least 20 target accounts across more than one region.
- Record the current HMI workflow, time, staffing, tool costs, and decision criteria.
- Obtain written interest in a specific evaluation, not general praise.

### Gate 2: conversion

- Track Alpha or Beta evaluations from first Demo through design-in decision.
- Define what counts as an evaluation, qualified opportunity, design-in, and production program.
- Compare Studio-assisted opportunities with a relevant baseline.

### Gate 3: economics

- Confirm annual hardware revenue, gross margin, support cost, AI cost, and sales cycle by cohort.
- Remove revenue overlap between engines.
- Test whether the US$2.0M, US$10K, and US$2.0M averages survive real contracts.

### Gate 4: repeatability

- Measure renewals, repeat orders, product variants, and later-generation retention.
- Confirm that results come from a process that regional teams and distributors can reproduce.

### Gate 5: scale

- Verify FAE capacity, training, localization, security reviews, cloud billing, support, supply, and quality.
- Define an owner and operating budget for every bottleneck.

## Sensitivity of Engine A

US$160M of hardware revenue can come from different mixes:

| Active programs | Average annual revenue per program | Hardware total |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 | US$4.0M | US$160M |
| 80 | US$2.0M | US$160M |
| 160 | US$1.0M | US$160M |

The mix affects sales capacity, concentration risk, support load, and supply planning. Finance and Sales must select a defendable portfolio assumption.

## Market scale context

[Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/human-machine-interface-market) projects a US$11.60B global HMI market in 2030. US$200M is about 1.7% of that figure.

[MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/artificial-intelligence-manufacturing-market-72679105.html) projects the AI in manufacturing market from US$34.18B in 2025 to US$155.04B in 2030.

These third-party estimates describe broad markets with their own definitions. They show scale only. They do not establish EDT's serviceable market, market share, timing, or revenue.

## Conflict with the internal investor-conference draft

The internal source material includes an illustrative hardware path reaching TWD2.0B in 2030. That draft and the US$200M North Star differ in currency, scale, possible scope, and approval status.

Before any external use, Finance and IR must decide:

- Whether the two models cover the same business.
- Whether US$200M refers to 2030, a later year, or an undated steady-state run-rate.
- Which exchange rate and reporting basis apply.
- Whether the service and licensing engines sit inside or outside the earlier hardware model.
- Which figure, if any, can appear in an external keynote.

Do not convert TWD2.0B into US dollars on stage and imply that the result supports US$200M.

## Safe keynote wording

> Here is the North Star: a repeatable US$200 million annual revenue engine. This is a scenario model, not approved guidance. The model has three parts: US$160 million from active production display programs, US$20 million from paid AI and enterprise services, and US$20 million from strategic agreements. Every line is an assumption. Our next job is to test those assumptions with customers, contracts, and operating data.

## Unsafe wording

- We will reach US$200M.
- The market data proves our forecast.
- Studio creates US$200M of ARR.
- Eighty customers are already committed.
- The free software guarantees hardware conversion.
- Beta will deliver this business model.
