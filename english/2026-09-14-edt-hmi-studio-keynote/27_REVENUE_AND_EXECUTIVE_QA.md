# Revenue and executive Q&A

## Answer pattern

Use three short moves:

1. **Answer:** Give the direct answer.
2. **Boundary or evidence:** State one fact, assumption, or limit.
3. **Next step:** Name the proof, owner, or follow-up.

Keep the first answer under 25 seconds. Add detail only when the executive asks.

## Product and strategy

### 1. What is EDT HMI Studio?

> EDT HMI Studio is a visual development environment for embedded touch interfaces. It brings design, logic, device communication, emulation, and deployment into one project. The current product also includes an AI assistant that can create or edit project content through the editor.

### 2. Why should EDT build software?

> Software can move EDT earlier in the customer's design process. We can help the customer create and validate the HMI around a supported EDT profile, then compete for the production display program. We must measure whether that path improves conversion and retention.

### 3. Is this just another GUI designer?

> The broader workflow is the difference. Studio connects the screen to visual logic, device values, generated-code emulation, and supported hardware. The proof is an interactive project that can continue from evaluation toward deployment.

### 4. Who is the first target customer?

> The initial target should be teams building embedded touch products that use serial device communication and need a faster evaluation path. We should begin with accounts where EDT already has hardware access and can measure the design-in decision.

### 5. What customer problem is most valuable?

> We believe late integration and repeated handoffs are expensive problems. The first pilot must quantify the customer's current time, staffing, and failure points. We should let evidence decide which problem becomes the primary paid value.

### 6. Does the product replace firmware engineers?

> No. Studio lets more work begin visually and reduces the amount that must start as handwritten application code. Firmware engineers still own special integration, target constraints, review, testing, and production quality.

### 8. Why does the source version differ from the public page?

> The development branch moves faster than the public evaluation package. We use the current source to verify engineering progress and the official page to describe public availability. I will not present an internal version as a public release.

### 9. Which boards are supported?

> The current source contains profiles for STM32F746G-DISCO, STM32H747I-DISCO, and EDT EVK043027B. Their capabilities differ, so we validate the exact board, protocol, memory, and media requirement before making a commitment.

### 10. Does it support CAN or Ethernet?

> Those are not current production paths. The source contains hardware definitions and research work, but the required runtime path is incomplete. Please tell me whether the use case needs raw CAN, CANopen, J1939, or Ethernet so we can evaluate the correct roadmap.

### 11. Does the AI always produce correct results?

> No AI system should receive that promise. The assistant creates or edits normal project content through the editor. A person reviews the result, can adjust it by hand, and can undo the applied AI change as one step.

### 12. Is customer data private?

> It depends on the provider and configuration. OpenRouter is an external service. Ollama can stay local when it points to a local host. Security, retention, model, region, and contract terms require an approved enterprise policy.

### 13. Can AI flash or operate the machine?

> The current assistant does not operate the target hardware. It works on project content inside the editor. Build, flash, and hardware validation remain controlled engineering steps.

## US$200M model

### 14. Is US$200M a forecast?

> No. It is a strategic North Star and a scenario model, not approved guidance. The model exposes three testable assumptions: US$160M from production display programs, US$20M from paid services, and US$20M from strategic agreements.

### 15. When will EDT reach US$200M?

> The model does not have an approved date. Finance and management must define the timing after we validate conversion, program value, service demand, and operating capacity. I will not attach a year before that work is complete.

### 16. Why US$200M?

> The number is large enough to force a business-system discussion instead of a feature discussion. Its value comes from the transparent equation behind it. Management can test, revise, or reject each assumption using customer and financial data.

### 17. How do you calculate it?

> The illustrative equation is 80 active production programs at US$2M each, 2,000 paid enterprise teams at US$10K each, and 10 strategic agreements at US$2M each. The total is US$200M in annual run-rate.

### 18. Why 80 production programs?

> Eighty is an assumption, not a current pipeline count. It produces US$160M at an average of US$2M per active program. Sales and Finance must test the number against account capacity, conversion, program life, concentration risk, and supply.

### 19. Why would one program produce US$2M per year?

> That is an illustrative portfolio average. Real programs will vary by display, annual volume, price, and product life. We need historical EDT cohorts and qualified opportunities to replace the assumption with a defendable distribution.

### 20. Is this recurring revenue?

> It is better described as repeatable annual revenue. Hardware repeats through production orders, services repeat through renewals, and strategic agreements may repeat through annual commitments. Only contracted recurring amounts should be reported as recurring revenue.

### 21. Is this software ARR?

> No. In the scenario, US$160M comes from production display hardware. Only the service and licensing portions could qualify as software or recurring contract revenue, depending on the final agreements and accounting treatment.

### 22. What about profit and gross margin?

> This model shows revenue only. It does not claim profit. Finance must add hardware margin, AI provider cost, support cost, sales cost, working capital, and investment before management can judge the economics.

### 23. Are you double counting revenue?

> The model is designed to keep the three lines separate, but real accounts may overlap. Finance must assign each contract and shipment to one category, remove internal transfers, and reconcile the model to reported revenue.

### 24. Your internal draft mentions TWD2.0B. Why is this US$200M?

> They are different unapproved scenarios. The TWD2.0B draft focused on a hardware path, while this keynote model adds possible services and strategic agreements at a larger scale. Finance and IR must reconcile scope, timing, currency, and disclosure before either figure is external.

### 25. Does market size prove the target?

> No. Third-party market estimates provide scale context only. They do not prove EDT's serviceable market, share, timing, or conversion. Customer contracts and operating data must support the target.

## Commercial model

### 26. Will Studio be free?

> We are evaluating models that can accelerate EDT hardware adoption, including a possible offer linked to compatible EDT-manufactured displays. The final free conditions, license, and regional terms require executive, Finance, Legal, and channel approval.

### 27. How will EDT make money from AI?

> Possible models include customer-funded provider usage or an EDT-managed enterprise service. We first need an approved data policy, provider cost, support model, price, gross margin, and billing process. No final offer has been approved.

### 28. What does the distributor earn?

> The channel economics are not approved yet. We need a model that rewards qualified evaluations, technical support, and production design-ins without creating price conflict. We will confirm margin, deal registration, and support duties in writing.

### 29. Will subsidiaries or headquarters own the deal?

> That requires a clear account and revenue-attribution policy. My recommendation is to define ownership by customer, region, technical contribution, and production shipment, then document it before the pilot begins.

### 30. Will partners receive regional exclusivity?

> We have no approved exclusivity commitment. Exclusivity would require performance thresholds, territory definitions, product scope, review dates, and termination rights. I will not make that promise in this meeting.

### 31. Will software cannibalize service or FAE revenue?

> It may change where FAE time is spent. The intended result is less repeated setup and more work on qualified integration and production programs. We should measure FAE hours per design-in and total account value.

### 32. Why would a customer pay after receiving the software?

> A customer pays when an enterprise service reduces risk or operating cost. Potential examples include managed AI, security controls, support commitments, training, or private deployment. We need willingness-to-pay evidence before choosing the offer.

### 33. What prevents a competitor from copying this?

> The defensible position would come from the complete workflow, EDT board knowledge, customer projects, channel execution, and repeated improvement. We should avoid claiming a legal moat until IP counsel reviews the code, licenses, and protectable assets.

## Execution

### 34. What happens in the first 90 days?

> We select focused target accounts, document their current workflow, run controlled evaluations, and track each path to board validation. In parallel, we test one paid service concept and begin discovery with a small number of strategic partners.

### 35. What is the most important metric?

> The first commercial metric is qualified evaluation to production design-in conversion. We also need cycle time, annual program value, margin, support load, repeat orders, and renewals to understand whether the model scales.

### 36. How much investment is required?

> The current materials do not contain an approved budget. Product, Engineering, Sales, FAE, Security, Support, and Finance must size the team and operating cost against stage-gated evidence. I will provide the approved plan after that review.

### 37. What is the biggest risk?

> The biggest business risk is adoption without conversion: customers may like the Demo but never nominate EDT hardware or pay for services. The pilot must measure the whole path from evaluation to commercial decision.

### 38. What if the assumptions fail?

> We change or stop the weak engine. The purpose of the model is to make assumptions visible early. A failed assumption becomes useful when we detect it before large investment.

### 39. How do we avoid channel conflict?

> Define account ownership, deal registration, pricing authority, support duties, data access, and revenue credit before scale. Then pilot the rules in a small number of regions and correct the model before broad rollout.

### 40. What decision do you want from us today?

> I am asking for approval to run a measured validation program, not approval of a US$200M forecast. We need named owners, target accounts, success metrics, a 90-day review date, and a clear claim-approval process.

## Rescue answers

> There are two parts. The current product fact is [fact]. The financial assumption still requires [owner] approval.

> The short answer is [yes / no / not yet / it depends on the board]. The current boundary is [one sentence].

### You hear the topic but miss a word

> I understood the main question, but I missed the last term. Could you repeat that part?

### You need to correct yourself

> Let me correct one number. The scenario is [correct number and unit]. It is an assumption, not approved guidance.

### Return to the main point

> Let me return to the main point. The next proof is [metric or validation].
