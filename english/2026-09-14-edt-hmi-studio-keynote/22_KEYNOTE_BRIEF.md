# 30-minute keynote brief

## Presentation objective

By the end of 30 minutes, an overseas subsidiary leader, distributor executive, or senior manager should be able to answer four questions:

1. What is EDT HMI Studio?
2. What can it prove today?
3. Why can it change how EDT wins and keeps display business?
4. Which assumptions would need to become true for a repeatable US$200M annual revenue engine?

## Audience

- General managers and vice presidents at overseas subsidiaries
- Owners and senior managers at distributors
- Product, sales, FAE, engineering, finance, and strategy leaders
- Mixed English proficiency and mixed technical depth

Assume the audience understands display modules and industrial customers, but does not know the Studio architecture.

## One-sentence message

> EDT HMI Studio turns an EDT display from a component that customers evaluate into a platform that helps them design, validate, deploy, and repeat complete HMI products.

## Two chapters

### Chapter 1: EDT HMI Studio

**Question:** What has EDT built?

**Answer:** A visual, AI-assisted workflow for embedded touch interfaces that connects design, logic, communication, emulation, and supported hardware.

**Proof:** Show one visible transformation. Begin with a sketch, sentence, or existing screen. Let the assistant create or edit the HMI. Then run the generated application in the Emulator or Customer Demo.

### Chapter 2: The US$200M revenue engine

**Question:** How could this product create repeatable annual revenue for EDT?

**Answer:** Studio can help EDT win more production display programs, keep customers through later product generations, add paid AI and support services, and open a limited number of strategic licensing relationships.

**Proof:** Reveal one transparent equation. Label every number as an assumption. Explain what EDT must validate next.

## Emotional arc

| Moment | Audience feeling | Speaker job |
|---|---|---|
| Old HMI workflow | Friction | Make the fragmented workflow familiar |
| Product name reveal | Curiosity | Use silence before the name |
| AI creates or edits a screen | Surprise | Show one clean change |
| Emulator or Customer Demo runs | Confidence | Prove the result is interactive |
| EDT business flywheel | Recognition | Connect software activity to hardware design-ins |
| US$200M reveal | Ambition | Hold the number for several seconds |
| Assumption slide | Trust | Explain exactly what remains unproven |
| Closing | Agency | Give leaders one next action |

The desired launch energy comes from pacing, contrast, proof, and silence. Do not imitate another company's wording or stage mannerisms.

## Language rules for a beginner speaker

- One idea per sentence.
- Most sentences under 14 words.
- Aim for an overall average near 95 words per minute. Slow the reveal lines and use up to 100 to 105 for explanatory sections.
- Pause after a number, product name, or question.
- Use everyday verbs: build, show, test, connect, run, win, keep.
- Use the exact same wording for the opening, US$200M caveat, and closing in every rehearsal.
- If a line is difficult after five attempts, simplify it. Do not increase speed.

## Stage rules

- Look at the audience for the first and last sentence of every section.
- Never read a table aloud.
- Let one visual carry one idea.
- Keep the screen dark or minimal before each major reveal.
- Do not speak while clicking through a risky Demo step.
- If the Demo fails, switch to the recorded proof image and continue within ten seconds.

## Demo choice

Preferred live proof:

1. Open a prepared project.
2. Ask the assistant for one visible, low-risk change.
3. Apply it and show one-step undo.
4. Run the result in Emulator or Customer Demo.

Avoid generating a full project live unless the exact prompt, model, network, latency, and fallback have been tested on the event machine.

## Success criteria

The presentation succeeds when:

- A non-engineer can explain the product in one sentence.
- A technical leader can identify the supported and unsupported paths.
- A commercial leader can repeat the three revenue engines.
- No listener mistakes US$200M for approved guidance.
- The room agrees on one validation action, owner, and date.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Do not announce Beta before the official page and internal approval change.
- Do not describe Alpha as a downstream customer release.
- Do not call the whole US$200M software ARR.
- Do not claim production CAN, Ethernet, or universal board support.
- Do not promise pricing, exclusivity, SLA, privacy terms, or a release date without approval.
- Do not turn third-party market forecasts into EDT revenue forecasts.
