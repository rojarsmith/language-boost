# 海外子公司與通路商問答

通路主管最在乎的不是 codegen，而是「賣給誰、怎麼 Demo、誰支援、誰拿錢、出了問題誰負責」。技術先答到足夠，然後把對話拉回合作模型。

## 市場與銷售

### 1. Why should we sell this product?

> It gives customers a clearer path from display evaluation to a working embedded interface. For a channel partner, it can make demonstrations, requirement qualification, and technical follow-up more repeatable.

### 2. Which customers should we target first?

> Start with embedded product or industrial equipment teams that use a supported display path and need Modbus RTU or serial commands. The first target should have a real project, a clear owner, and a bounded proof of concept.

### 3. Which opportunities should we reject or postpone?

> Postpone opportunities that require an unsupported board, production CAN or Ethernet, several simultaneous connections, or an undefined custom protocol unless an engineering scope is approved first.

### 4. What is the sales message?

> One project for screen design, behavior, device communication, generated-code validation, and deployment. The business value is faster evaluation, fewer handoffs, and earlier integration feedback.

### 5. How do we demonstrate it?

> Use a prepared example, not a blank project. Show Design, Protocol, Emulator, and Deploy in three to seven minutes. Then qualify the customer's board, protocol, role, and screen requirements.

### 6. What proof points can we use?

> The current source includes three board profiles, 25 design-palette components, implemented Modbus RTU and serial-command paths, a real-LVGL generated-code Emulator, and a multilingual Coffee Machine example. Do not turn those into performance limits or commercial commitments.

### 7. Can we bundle it with EDT display hardware? `[CONFIRM]`

> Technically, the workflow is well suited to a hardware-plus-software evaluation story. The bundle SKU, license, included support, and revenue allocation require an approved commercial plan.

### 8. Can we use it to sell custom displays?

> It can support the evaluation and HMI-development conversation, but a custom display or board still needs an explicit hardware profile and firmware integration. We should scope that engineering work before promising a delivery date.

## Enablement 與支援

### 16. What training will our team receive? `[CONFIRM]`

> I recommend three tracks: a short sales story, a guided demo and qualification track for pre-sales, and a technical deployment track for support engineers. The delivery format and certification policy still need approval.

### 17. Will you provide local-language material? `[CONFIRM]`

> The product itself supports multilingual project text, and the current materials include English and Traditional Chinese documentation. The official list of localized sales, training, and support content must be agreed by region.

### 18. Who handles first-line support? `[CONFIRM]`

> That must be explicit in the partner model. A practical structure is local first-line qualification, regional application support, and product-engineering escalation, but response targets and ownership require approval.

### 19. What information should first-line support collect?

> Product version, operating system, project file, target board, connector, protocol, device role, toolchain status, exact steps, logs, and whether the problem appears in Design, Emulator, build, or hardware.

### 20. What if the customer's board is not supported?

> Do not call it a software bug. Record the MCU, display interface, resolution, memory, touch controller, connectors, protocol, programmer, and expected volume. Then request a board-enablement assessment.

### 21. What if a customer requests CAN or Ethernet?

> Qualify the exact protocol and role first. CAN could mean raw signals, CANopen, or J1939; Ethernet could mean Modbus TCP or another socket protocol. These are not current production paths, so they require roadmap or engineering approval.

### 22. How do we handle custom features?

> Separate a product gap from a customer-specific request. Write the use case, acceptance criteria, reusable market value, target date, and commercial owner. Do not promise it from a live meeting.

### 23. Will you provide marketing development funds? `[CONFIRM]`

> No approved MDF policy is included in this package. We can propose activities and expected outcomes, but funding and reimbursement rules need written approval.

### 24. Can we use the screenshots and teaser deck?

> The repository contains generated marketing assets, but external usage rights, brand rules, version disclaimers, and launch claims should be confirmed before publication. Use only the approved final files.

### 25. Can we translate the product claims ourselves?

> Translate the approved message, not the raw README. Keep technical scope words such as current, supported, planned, and experimental. Product management should review any local claim before publication.

## 經營與績效

### 26. What sales target do you expect? `[CONFIRM]`

> The target should follow the agreed territory, addressable accounts, price model, enablement level, and launch timing. I do not have an approved number to give today.

### 27. How should we measure the first six months?

> Track trained sellers, qualified opportunities, completed demos, proof-of-concept starts, proof-of-concept success rate, conversion to paid projects, and support effort per opportunity. Revenue alone will not show where the channel process is failing.

### 28. What does a qualified opportunity look like?

> It has a named customer project, target board or MCU, display requirements, protocol specification, decision process, technical owner, commercial owner, timeline, and an agreed proof-of-concept outcome.

### 29. What should a joint account plan contain?

> Customer goal, use case, current workflow, target hardware, protocol, stakeholders, proof-of-concept scope, success criteria, commercial path, support owner, and next meeting date.

### 30. What is the first action after this meeting?

> Nominate one sales contact, one pre-sales contact, and one realistic customer use case. We will run a qualification session and decide whether it fits the current product scope.
